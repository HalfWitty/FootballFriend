Data. Scores, kickoff times and closing sportsbook lines (spread, total, moneyline and their prices) come from nflverse's public games file. Player and team box scores come from the Sleeper stats files in this repo's data/ folder (2023 onward), including Sleeper's TEAM rows (total yards, first downs, turnovers).

Expected points. Raw points are noisy - a pick-six or a missed field goal swings them. A regression fitted on past seasons turns each game's yards, first downs and turnovers into expected points. Ratings use 70% actual points and the rest expected points.

Ratings. Every team gets an offense and a defense number from a weighted regression (points = league average + offense + opponent's defense + home field), so scoring 30 on a great defense counts for more than 30 on a bad one. A game's weight halves every 6 weeks, last season counts 35% as much and fades as this season builds, and every rating is mixed with 4 games of league-average play so one blowout doesn't take over. Home field is fitted from the data each week; neutral-site games get none.

Margin and totals. NFL scores bunch on 3 and 7, so the margin isn't a smooth bell curve. For a projected margin, the model looks up how real games with a similar closing spread finished (2010 on), which keeps the key numbers - that's where the push percentages come from. Totals use a bell curve with the historical spread of totals (about 13 points). Team totals use a 9-point spread around each team's projection.

Player props. Each player's per-game rate is a recency-weighted average of the games he played (half-life 5 games; last season counts half). It's adjusted by how much this week's opponent allows to his position (shrunk toward average) and by game script: a team projected to score more than usual throws, runs and scores a bit more. Yardage uses a bell curve whose width was fitted on 2024-2025 misses; receptions and touchdowns use Poisson counts. Anytime TD chances are pulled toward average because raw TD rates over a few games are mostly luck. QBs: the feed's listed starter is used when present.

Fair odds and Bet at. Fair odds are the no-vig price of the model's probability (with pushes removed). Bet at knocks the probability down by a cushion (3.5 points for sides and totals, 6 for props, plus 1.5 more through week 6), so a price at or better than it is still a good bet if the model is somewhat off.

Top bets. A high-probability bet (58%+) qualifies if the price beats fair odds; any other bet needs the cautious probability to beat the price. No longshots (model under 30%) and nothing more than 8 points away from the market's no-vig probability - in the NFL a big disagreement with the closing line usually means the model is missing news (injuries, a QB change, weather). Qualifying bets alternate between high-probability picks and the biggest edges.

Track record. Each run rebuilds every earlier week of the season walk-forward and grades it, plus three full past seasons under the same rules, and logs every prediction to nfl_data/predictions_log.csv. Check the calibration and the long-run table before trusting a market.

Injuries and rosters. For games not yet played, each run reads current teams, injury designations and depth charts from Sleeper and ESPN's injury report. Players who are out, on IR, doubtful or now on another team are dropped from the projections, and their targets and carries go to the teammates who are left. The starting QB is the healthy QB atop the depth chart. When he isn't the QB the team's rating was built on, the projected score moves by about 3.5 points per net yard per dropback between the two (fit on 2024-2026 games, where the full effect was about 4.7). Other injuries change player projections but not the team rating.

Limits. The model doesn't know about weather or rest, and past weeks in the track record are rebuilt without injury data (it isn't known for past dates). It only sees closing lines, which are the hardest to beat. Re-run close to kickoff for late scratches, and shop for the best price.

Settings live at the top of nfl_edge.py under CONFIG; grade, trend and unit settings live in edge_common.py under SETTINGS.

Grades, trends and units

Grades. Every pick gets a score out of 100: edge at the price (30), win chance (10), how close the model is to the market (10; a huge gap usually means news the model can't see), whether situational trends back the side (30) and how much the model's claimed edge has held up in that bet type (20). A is 55+, B 44+, C 34+, D below. Unpriced picks and early-season picks top out at B. Hover a grade for the breakdown.

Trend picks. Each side is checked for situational factors: a better record, scoring margin or recent form, a home/road split, the opponent on a back-to-back or coming off overtime, rest, streaks and the opponent's last loss being to a worse team. Each factor's record comes from past games, and only factors that won more than half the time (after mixing in 60 games of 50/50) count. A trend pick needs 3+ of them and a net edge over the opponent's factors. The "ways to bet it" ladder compares the moneyline, regulation or spread and team total against the model's fair price, and the play is the one with the best expected return.

Suggested units. 0.25 Kelly (1 unit = 1% of a bankroll), rounded to half units and capped at 3u. Before sizing, the model's win chance is pulled toward the market by the bet type's trust: trust = the share of the model's claimed edge that has actually shown up in graded results, starting at 50% and updating as games finish. A bet type that has matched the market and not the model gets small or zero units.

Scatter. On the Track record tab every priced pick is placed by its edge at the price (across) and the price (up), coloured by result, with upcoming picks as rings. Zones shade areas where at least 5 graded picks made or lost money, so you can see whether tonight's picks sit where past ones paid.
